Know Before You Go

What to expect.

From your first consultation to ongoing follow-ups, here's how care actually works — and what to know before you begin. Every therapy is prescription-only and compounded by a licensed U.S. pharmacy.

A real conversation, not a sales pitch

Your first visit is about your health picture — goals, history, current medications, and lifestyle. Nothing is prescribed until the physician determines a therapy is appropriate for you.

Bring your questions

Curious how a compound works, what the research shows, or how it might fit alongside what you're already doing? This is the time to ask everything.

Labs where they matter

Depending on your goals, baseline lab work may be recommended so your protocol starts from real data rather than assumptions.

No obligation

If peptide therapy isn't the right fit, you'll hear that honestly — along with what might serve you better.

Your prescription, compounded fresh

Once prescribed, your therapy is prepared by a licensed U.S. compounding pharmacy and shipped to your door with storage and handling instructions.

Clear administration guidance

You'll receive step-by-step instruction on how your therapy is taken or administered, and support is available whenever questions come up.

Timelines are individual

Some people notice changes within weeks; for others it takes longer. Your follow-ups exist to track what's actually happening for you.

Consistency matters most

Peptide protocols tend to reward consistency. Your plan will include a realistic schedule designed to fit your life.

Physician oversight throughout

Your protocol isn't set-and-forget. The prescribing physician remains involved, and adjustments are made based on your response.

Know the common side effects

Most reported side effects are mild — things like temporary injection-site redness or nausea with certain compounds. You'll be told what's routine and what isn't before you begin.

When to reach out

You'll get clear guidance on which symptoms warrant a call to the practice and which warrant prompt medical care. When in doubt, reach out.

Quality you can trace

Compounds come exclusively from a licensed, U.S.-accredited compounding pharmacy — never gray-market or "research use" suppliers.
